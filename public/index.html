Choosing your activity level

  • Sedentary: desk job, mostly driving, little or no exercise. Under ~5,000 steps a day.
  • Light: 1–3 sessions a week, e.g. gym twice and a weekend walk. ~5,000–7,500 steps.
  • Moderate: 3–5 sessions a week, e.g. gym 3× plus a run or class. ~7,500–10,000 steps.
  • Very active: 6–7 hard sessions a week, or daily training plus an on-your-feet job.
  • Athlete: physical job plus training, or training twice a day.
A session is 30–60 min that gets your heart rate up (you can talk, but not sing): gym, running, cycling, swimming, sports, classes or a brisk 45–60 min walk. A stroll or a 15-minute workout doesn't count.
Unsure? Pick the lower level. If you sit most of the day, go one step lower, and move up if you lose weight faster than predicted.

    Basal calories (BMR) are what your body burns at complete rest, estimated from your sex, age, height and weight with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Maintenance is BMR times your activity level. You eat the same calories every day, so weight loss slows gently as your body gets lighter and burns less.

    This is an estimate built from the figures you entered, not medical advice. It assumes about 7,700 kcal per kg of body weight (3,500 per lb) and recalculates maintenance every day as your weight falls. Real loss is rarely a straight line: water, glycogen and metabolic adaptation make weekly numbers bounce around, and long-term loss is usually a little slower than this model shows. Once the goal is reached the projection switches to maintenance. For very low intakes or medical conditions, plan with a doctor or registered dietitian.
